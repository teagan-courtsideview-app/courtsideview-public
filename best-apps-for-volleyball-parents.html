2027 season prep

Your volleyball match-day checklist

Know where to go, who is keeping score, and how everyone else will follow. Use this checklist before your first tournament, then repeat the match-day checks each weekend.

Prepared for the 2027 season on October 26, 2026. Check your team’s and event’s current requirements before play. The app comparisons below retain their separate source-review dates.

Before the season starts

  • Find the team’s source for schedules. Ask your coach or team manager where court assignments, arrival times, work-team duties and last-minute changes will appear. Save that destination on every attending adult’s phone.
  • Choose the scoring job. Decide whether you want a family scoreboard, player-level stats or the event’s official scorekeeping record. Ask the event what system and training it requires for official duties.
  • Choose one person to keep the family score. Agree who will start each match and who can take over if that person needs to leave. A live score is useful only when someone keeps it current.
  • Prepare supporters at home. Confirm whether they want score updates, live video or both. Send account instructions before tournament morning and test the browser link together.
  • Practise once. Try a short practice match so the scorer can find point entry, correction, timeout and end-set actions. Check that team names are easy to distinguish on the screen.

The night before

  • Charge the scoring phone and any separate broadcasting device. Pack the charging cable and a charged power bank.
  • Recheck the venue address, arrival time, parking instructions and first court assignment in the team’s chosen schedule source.
  • Confirm the person who will score and the backup contact. Keep the plan simple enough to explain in one message.
  • If video is planned, check team and event filming rules, device placement and who will run the camera. Score sharing and video broadcasting are separate tasks.

Before first serve

  • Check the latest court and start-time information.
  • Confirm the two team names and the match format supplied by the event. If you are on official scorer duty, use the required materials and trained workflow.
  • Check the phone battery and your connection. Local scoring and online sharing have different connection needs; test the actual sharing connection at the court.
  • Start the correct match. If using CourtsideView, use Scorekeeper for a score-focused match or the appropriate registered-account tools when player stats and connected features are needed.
  • Open the shared view on a second device before sending it to supporters. Check the team names, set and current score.

During the match

Record each confirmed point promptly. Compare the score with the court scoreboard at natural pauses and resolve discrepancies before they grow. If you miss a rally, ask for the confirmed score instead of guessing. Keep the scorer’s screen focused on the match; use another adult for messages or camera work when possible.

After the final point

Confirm the final result before ending the match. Tell supporters that play has finished and check where the next court or start-time update will appear. CourtsideView FanView links expire 15 minutes after the game concludes, so treat them as live match links rather than permanent archives.

The winning parent app reduces questions, not just taps.

The parent problem is not technology. It is uncertainty: court changes, schedule shifts, hidden scoreboards, missed livestreams, and the group chat asking what happened after every point.

A strong parent app answers the questions people actually ask during a volleyball weekend: Where are we playing? What is the score? Who is serving? How did my player do? Can family follow from home?

Common questions.

What is the best volleyball app for parents at tournaments?

Consider CourtsideView when parents need live match scoring, a visible scoreboard, and FanView sharing. TeamSnap, SportsEngine, AES, and GameChanger can be useful depending on what the team or tournament uses for schedules and communication.

Can family follow CourtsideView without installing the app?

Yes. FanView lets family follow live scores and match activity in a browser without installing CourtsideView. Viewing and broadcasting require registered accounts. Live sharing needs internet, and video needs someone broadcasting.

What if I only want to track score for myself?

Use CourtsideView Scorekeeper. It is fast enough for simple point-by-point scoring, but you are not boxed into a score-only tool if your team later wants rosters, Courtside mode, player stats, FanView sharing.

How can grandparents follow a volleyball match?

Ask the scorekeeper for the FanView link and open it in a browser with an internet connection. Someone at the match must keep the score current. Live scores do not guarantee live video; video depends on a broadcaster going live.
