Source-based workflow report

Seven volleyball match-day questions, answered from the product sources

Keeping score, tracking player actions and following from another location are different jobs. This report maps seven practical questions to capabilities described on CourtsideView’s website and public app listing.

Published and reviewed October 26, 2026 · CourtsideView editorial team

What this report contains
  • Seven documented product workflows
  • Sources and limits for each answer
  • Clearly hypothetical example responses

Product-source analysis. No participant survey or interviews were conducted.

1. What do I need if my job is simply to keep the score?

Start with the scoreboard task: team names, current points, set progress and timeouts. CourtsideView's public app listing describes a score-only match that does not require a roster. That gives a clear starting point when player-level tracking is outside your role.

Evidence limit: A product description does not establish how long setup takes or how easily every user will learn it. We did not run timed usability trials.

Source: CourtsideView on the App Store

2. What changes when I want player statistics as well?

Player tracking needs more context than a running total. CourtsideView's player-stats page describes a workflow that connects recorded player actions with rosters, the on-court lineup, rotations and the live score. Prepare the relevant player information before the match if this is the job you plan to do.

Evidence limit: Recorded stats depend on what the person tracking the match enters. This report does not test stat accuracy or claim that an app can replace a trained observer.

Source: Volleyball player stats

3. Can someone follow the score from another location?

CourtsideView documents FanView as a shared web page for match scores and activity. This gives supporters a browser destination while the scorer continues recording the match. Share access details before first serve and explain who will keep the information current. FanView viewing and broadcasting require registered accounts; browser viewers do not need to install the app.

Evidence limit: Live sharing needs an active match publisher and an internet connection. A link alone does not make the score update itself.

Source: Volleyball scorekeeping and FanView sharing

4. Is live-score sharing the same thing as broadcasting video?

No. The public app listing describes live scores, activity and optional live video as separate FanView capabilities. Decide whether supporters need a scoreboard, a camera view, or both before assigning match-day responsibilities.

Evidence limit: Optional video is not proof that every venue, device or connection will support a reliable broadcast. This report does not test streaming quality, recording or long-term video storage.

Source: CourtsideView on the App Store

5. What should I plan for when gym internet is unreliable?

The public app listing says core scoring can continue in limited-connectivity conditions and that sharing is optional. Treat keeping your local score and delivering updates to remote supporters as separate responsibilities. Test the connection before the match and agree on a fallback communication plan.

Evidence limit: This is a product-documentation review, not a network or offline recovery test. It does not guarantee that a particular phone, gym or reconnection sequence will work.

Source: CourtsideView on the App Store

6. Does a family scoring app replace the event's official scoresheet?

Ask the event organizer which scoring process is accepted. The existing CourtsideView scoring guide distinguishes family scorekeeping from formal event scoring tools. A personal match record and the official event record can serve different purposes.

Evidence limit: This report does not certify CourtsideView, or any other tool, for a league, school, region or tournament. The event's current requirements control the official record.

Source: Volleyball scorekeeping app guide

7. What happens to a FanView link after the match?

The public app listing states that the match's stable FanView link expires 15 minutes after the game concludes. Plan around it as a live match-sharing destination. Tell supporters when the match is starting rather than presenting the link as a permanent archive.

Evidence limit: This report does not establish a permanent match-video or postgame archive. Review the current product's save and export options separately if those are part of your requirements.

Source: CourtsideView on the App Store

  • Correcting a point: both explained how to Undo an immediately misplaced point, award it to the correct team and check both totals. The instructions do not promise that every event can be undone. Read the correction walkthrough.
  • Interpreting player actions: both identified the fictional dig, assist and kill as contributions to one team point, using Courtside mode with a registered account. They distinguished the example from a measured result or exported report. Read the player-tracking walkthrough.
  • Preparing remote family: both found the account and internet requirements, browser viewing without an app install, optional video that needs a broadcaster, and the game link’s expiry 15 minutes after conclusion. Read the family walkthrough.
